## Core Philosophy

- **Cash decays, equities don't.** Money in a bank loses to inflation over any horizon I care about. Equity ownership is the only vehicle I trust to outrun it.
- **I'm paid for sitting through the falls.** The premium over T-bills isn't free — it's compensation for holding assets that can drop 40% while I do nothing. If I sell in the drop, I've paid the price and refused the payment.
- **Time in the market beats anything.** This is the belief. Every rule below exists to stop me trading against it.
- **Top-down, not name-by-name.** My reading is macro and geopolitics. I don't have time to build a thesis on every holding and I won't pretend otherwise.
- **Macro is a disaster check, not an allocation tool.** Rates, policy and geopolitics tell me whether something is structurally broken. Absent idiosyncratic damage, they don't make me trade.
- **A theme is not a position.** I believe macro themes — AI in particular — drive returns. But a theme without a size, a horizon, and a falsification condition is just an opinion I paid for.
- **Long-only core, momentum only as an add-signal.** I add to things I already own when they're working. I don't chase names I don't hold. *(The SpaceX short violates this. See Live Contradictions.)*
- **Two books, one wall.** The investment portfolio and the trading account run on different mindsets and never fund each other after a loss.
- **The cash book has its own rule.** The savings balance is held for its yield and reviewed when the rate moves — not when the market falls. It is not dry powder, and I've decided that deliberately.
- **My edge is not speed.** I have no information, execution or infrastructure advantage. Any strategy requiring me to be faster or better-informed than the market is one I lose at.
- **Hype is my most expensive input.** Figma at IPO. Every IPO before it. SpaceX now. Each arrived the same way: something was loud, and I acted. Loudness is now a reason to wait, not a reason to act.
- **Rules written after the loss are not rules.** They're relief. Every rule here is binding today, not from the day my current position closes.

## Mindset Rules

1. **72-hour cooldown on anything new. Seven days if it arrived via hype.** If I first heard of it this week, three days minimum. If it reached me through momentum, a news cycle, a listing event or social chatter, seven. This exists because of Figma. No exceptions for "the move will be gone" — if it's gone in a week, it was never mine.
2. **Never top up the trading account after a loss.** Not the same day, not the same month. The wall between the books is one-directional and permanent.
3. **The cash book does not rescue the equity book.** A 30% market fall is not a reason to deploy it. Only a change in the savings rate opens that decision.
4. **Contributions stop when income stops.** No feeding the portfolio out of the cash pile during the job-hunt gap.
5. **30% of salary in, automatically, once employed.** Resumes with the first HK paycheque, not before.
6. **The stop is set before the position opens, and it fires without my permission.** Entered with the trade. I do not move it down.
7. **No leverage outside the trading account. Ever.** A leveraged position in a forty-year portfolio is a contradiction.
8. **If I hesitate at execution, I'm too big.** Hesitation on the lot size is my most honest signal — a body signal, not a judgement call. When I feel it, I cut the size. I do not talk myself past it.
9. **No new position while an existing one is underwater and unstopped.** I don't get to open the next trade until I've dealt with the last one.
10. **Macro doesn't make me trade. It only makes me look.** Nothing gets sold without a specific, name-level reason.
11. **If I can't say why I own it in one sentence, I don't add to it.** New capital goes to positions I can defend, or nowhere.
12. **A friend's conviction is not my thesis, and a friend's employer is not either.** People have moved me before. If I want to support a friend, that's a gift and I call it one. **Combined ceiling on anything socially sourced: 2% of capital.**
13. **A deadline is an automatic no.** A closing window is a reason to decline, not a reason to hurry. This already works — protect it.
14. **Three tactical trades per month, hard ceiling.** Any month I exceed it, the trading account is frozen for the following month. This exists specifically for the period from September when I have no job and unlimited screen time.
15. **When I want to tell someone about a position, I write it down instead.** The journal absorbs the reassurance-seeking before it turns into a trade.
16. **Judge the book against the index I could have bought instead.** If the gap is embarrassing and persistent, the answer is to own the index, not to trade harder.
17. **No new position while the position count is above 30.** Adding a 75th holding to a book I can give 1.6 minutes a week is not investing. Until the tail is dealt with, the only permitted transactions are exits and consolidations.
18. **Every position gets a written one-line reason, or it goes on the exit list.** The 47 names bought on 2026-04-30 are the test case. If I can't say why I own it, I don't.
19. **Thematic exposure gets measured quarterly, not felt.** Semis 21.5% and AI 31% were both surprises to me. A theme I haven't sized is a theme that's sizing itself.

## Anti-Portfolio

- **Crypto and digital assets.** Any form, any wrapper, any friend, any track record. A 48-hour window is a reason to decline, not to move.
- **Meme coins.** Zero tolerance, no size, no exception.
- **Penny stocks.** No liquidity, no floor, no way to verify anything with the time I have.
- **Anything sourced from group chats, unsolicited tips or DMs.**
- **Anything I first heard about this week.** Blocked 72 hours minimum, 7 days if it arrived via hype.
- **Anything with a decision deadline attached.**
- **Any fund whose underlying holdings I have not checked against what I already own.**
- **Anything funded by topping up the trading account after a loss.** The trade may be right; the funding is the tell.
- **Any position needing more attention than 2 hours a week can give it.** If it needs daily management, it isn't mine to own.
- **Anything I'd justify with "everyone's in it."** That's a crowd, not a reason.
- **Anything bought because someone I know works there, above the 2% social ceiling** — and if it's really about the friendship, it's a gift, not a position.
- **No shorts outside the trading account,** and none anywhere without a mechanical stop.
